Project: Kerala Vision

Livelihoods

From the PLFS data of 2017-18 to 2023-24, we use the data points on “Percentage distribution of usually working persons (ps+ss) by industry of work (industry sections of NIC-2008) for each State/ UT”. Basically it shows, for each Indian State and UT, the percentage of working people (who work either as their main job or as a side job) distributed across different industries as defined under NIC-2008.

  1. Agriculture is the primary employer in rural Kerala.

    From the data, employment in agriculture seems to be increasing in the last 7 years, especially among women. This goes against what I believed to be, that people are moving out of agriculture. In fact, urban female employment in agriculture has increased 3x (9.64 to 22.19), while rural female employment in agri has increased 2x (26.09 to 47.29)!

  2. Breadth of livelihoods for rural women in Kerala

    Some really interesting stuff here: Agriculture has increased sharply, manufacturing has largely remained constant. Women’s employment in construction has declined the most. There’s also notable declines in education & human, health & social work. Occupation in house-help has increased since the pandemic. Administrative & support service activities have increased.

  3. Breadth of livelihoods for urban women in Kerala

    We can see by comparison that a lot less urban women participate in agriculture, as compared to rural women (less than half). Manufacturing work for women is more urban. Construction has fallen sharply, as in rural, but it is more in urban. A more substantial number of women in urban areas are in finance & insurance, though this has also increased. Similarly, more urban women in education & human, health & social work, even though it has declined similarly to rural areas. Women as house help is more in urban than in rural.

  4. Breadth of livelihoods for rural men in Kerala

    Rural men are almost equally occupied in agriculture & construction, as opposed to women who are far more in agriculture than in construction. Surprisingly, construction hasn’t declined for men. Manufacturing has declined for men, while it has stayed consistent for women. Wholesale & retail trade has increased for men, transportation & storage has decreased. Barely any men are in the education & social sector. Less men than women in rural Kerala are in finance & insurance.

  5. Breadth of livelihoods for urban men in Kerala

    The most interesting data to me - barely any changes in the last 7 years. Urban men are half as likely to be in agriculture as rural men. They are twice as much in wholesale & retail trade. Urban men in manufacturing are decreasing, whereas urban women in manufacturing are the same across the years. Urban Men’s share in hospitality is increasing, whereas it is falling in transportation & storage. More urban men are in real estate than rural men, but the share is low.

Final takeaways:

  1. The pandemic seems to have pushed both rural and urban women in agriculture, largely from construction, education, finance & health-social work.
  2. Men are primarily engaged in wholesale & retail trade & repair work.
  3. Women are primarily engaged in agriculture, manufacturing, construction (declining), trade, education, health & social work.
  4. Both urban & rural men work in construction. Their participation has barely changed even after the pandemic, whereas women have disappeared from construction. Maybe the demand for women in construction is being fulfilled by migrants. Maybe construction as an industry has shrunk.
  5. Participation in manufacturing for rural men has decreased, but has stayed the same for women.
